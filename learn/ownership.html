Ownership & Governance

How members truly own and control a financial cooperative through democratic structures.

In a commercial bank you are a customer. In a credit union or financial cooperative you are an owner. This single difference changes everything about how decisions are made, who the institution answers to, and how surplus is used.

Core Idea

Ownership is tied to membership, not to the amount of money you deposit. Every adult member in good standing has exactly one vote — this is called one member, one vote.

1. Member Ownership

When you join a credit union you usually buy a membership share. That share makes you an owner.

  • Right to attend the annual general meeting (AGM)
  • Vote in elections for the Board of Directors
  • Stand for election yourself (subject to rules)
  • Share in benefits through better rates, lower fees, and possible patronage refunds

2. One Member, One Vote

A member with $200 in savings has the same voting power as a member with $2,000,000. Proxy voting is generally not allowed in federal credit unions in the United States. This prevents concentration of power.

3. Board of Directors

  • Odd number of directors (min 5, max 15 in US federal CUs)
  • Mostly volunteers
  • Sets strategy, oversees management, determines rates
  • Meets at least monthly
  • Fiduciary duty to the entire membership

4. Supervisory Committee

Independent oversight of controls, financial statements, and compliance. Acts as a channel for member concerns.
